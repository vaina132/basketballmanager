## Implemented: offseason contract buyouts

- Managers can review and buy out their own next-season contracts during the competitive offseason. The provisional settlement pays all remaining effective future salary once. The review shows the exact cost, salary budget freed, next-season squad size and Remaining budget after payment; confirmation is explicit.
- Release metadata and a single cash debit commit atomically. Original contracts and current-season registrations, payroll, results and appearances remain intact. Released future terms no longer consume salary capacity, but existing transfer installments remain payable. Other clubs can recruit the player through the ordinary dated offer process. The releasing club cannot re-sign the player during that window, including through delegated recruitment.
- Cash checks preserve current wages, agreed unpaid transfer fees and pending offers. Ordinary unpaid costs or overdue transfer fees block release. Open offers involving that player must be resolved first; salary reserves must still allow eight players, and the window must leave time for a replacement reply. Buyouts are explicit manager decisions; AI recruitment uses the same availability and no-rehire rules, without automatically spending on releases.
- Format 17 retains immutable buyouts with exact contract and negotiation history anchors. Validation replays the settlement command, released terms and debit at the historical cash prefix, rejecting altered compensation, dates, ownership, anchors and missing records. Formats 1-16 validate before migration and receive empty buyout history; format-16 schedules and partial receipts are preserved exactly.
- This release applies to next-season employment only. Midseason registration, negotiated compensation, contractual release clauses, agent fees and jurisdiction-specific employment rules remain open.

## Implemented: transfer installments and cash-flow protection

- Paid offers support **Pay in full**, **2 payments** (half now, half next opening day), and **3 payments** (half now, a quarter at opening, a quarter at matchday six). The review uses actual simulated dates and amounts. The complete agreed fee stays reserved; future seller receipts do not become spending money before payment.
- A completed transfer retains its frozen schedule and actual payment receipts. The initial payment commits with the new contract. Due installments run automatically at calendar boundaries using available cash after protected player wages. A shortfall produces a partial receipt and visible overdue balance; later real income can settle it. An unpaid transfer balance is kept separate from operating/wage arrears and is never credited to the seller in advance.
- Remaining budget, staff hiring and new transfer offers respect agreed unpaid fees as well as pending offers. Salary budgets remain separate. Transfers shows actual upcoming/overdue amounts and a detailed payment schedule without changing registration or match outcomes.
- Format 16 checks schedule arithmetic and dates, chronological receipt identities, exact paired cash entries, historical payroll reserves, affordable partial payments and mandatory calendar settlement boundaries. Market replay freezes old transfers' receipt prefixes, retaining opening-day/active-season payments without allowing future income to fund earlier proposals. Optional manual wage prepayments retain their existing timing; they do not simulate another calendar advance.
- Formats 1-15 validate before migration. Every previously completed paid transfer receives one upfront schedule and paid receipt using its existing ledger identity; original money, contracts and match results remain unchanged. Genuine archived format-15 data from an eleven-season career containing 29 transfers passed migration and round-trip checks.
- The development calendar and fee policy remain provisional. Loans, midseason registration changes, agent/signing fees, richer clauses, longer-term cash-flow forecasts and board recovery policies remain open.

## Implemented: club consent and paid offseason transfers

- Transfers now includes players contracted to other clubs for the following season. A paid proposal reserves its fee, annual salary and squad place. The seller replies first; an accepted club proposal schedules the player's decision one day later. The original seven-day expiry and window deadline still apply. Club review, fee counters, approval and player response remain separate saved states.
- The managed club receives an incoming-offer inbox with acceptance, rejection and fee-counter actions. Delegated and AI sellers use the same commands and replacement guards; their decisions are ordinary journal events. A provisional asking fee equals remaining contracted salary rounded up to EUR 1,000, within the supported EUR 1,000-500,000 range. This is a fictional financial policy, without unmodeled personality or hidden-skill valuation.
- A player's acceptance atomically records the new employment agreement, immutable release references to the seller's future terms, the accepted player response and one equal debit/credit pair. Approval alone makes no payment. Losing, withdrawn, expired, rejected or replaced proposals release their fee holds. Current-season payroll, registrations, completed matches and earlier contract terms are preserved; next season's payroll uses the new employer and accepted salary.
- Remaining budget and staff affordability both respect pending transfer fees. Salary allocations remain separate. Selling clubs protect replacement salary room, including concurrent approved sales, and retain a day for recruitment when a sale would leave fewer than eight signed players. A player cannot be transferred twice in one offseason. AI clubs may approach one affordable stronger known paid replacement per offseason after a sale, starting at 90% of the asking fee and responding to an affordable fee counter. Ordinary free recruitment remains their default.
- Format 15 validates the complete transfer, contract-release and financial history. Market replay starts from the actual prior contract, transfer and ledger prefixes, so later income cannot fund earlier offers. Formats 1-14 validate before migration and receive empty transfer history and null proposal metadata without changing recorded outcomes.

## Implemented: competing club offers

- Several clubs can submit live offers for the same unsigned next-season player. Each club independently reserves its proposed annual salary and squad place; replacing or withdrawing a bid changes only that club's hold. The player remains approachable while considering rival bids.
- On the scheduled reply date, acceptable due offers are compared by annual salary, longer contract, staying with the current employer, then a stable tie-break. Exactly one contract signs. Other live bids become **Chose another club**, link to the accepted offer and release their reservations. A lost pending bid creates no invented salary-negotiation response; an earlier counter keeps its original response history. Sporting randomness is unchanged.
- Low proposals still receive the existing counter/rejection decisions when no acceptable bid is due. Competitive budget checks also reserve any shortfall below the minimum-squad allowance for pending slots, protecting a club's ability to complete its squad when a bid loses.
- On the final proposal day, automatic clubs below eight signed players withdraw losing or unacceptable holds and offer ordinary terms to unopposed alternatives. Replies still occur on the closing date; no club receives an instant signing or extra recruitment day. If the manual squad would remain incomplete, Continue preserves the previous state and identifies the club needing players.
- Format 15 replays player choices and checks every winning link, released hold and accepted contract. Format-13 records first validate under their original single-offer rules. Existing live offers keep those rules until settled; competing bids then activate automatically at an empty-offer journal boundary. Historical windows, accepted contracts, finances and matches remain unchanged.
- This is a provisional financial choice policy for the fictional league. Personality, ambition, agents, reputation preferences, bonuses and negotiated release clauses remain unimplemented; club consent, paid transfers and offseason buyouts are described above.

## Implemented: automatic checkpoints and recovery

Employment/scouting activation, accepted offers, Manual/Assistant changes, scouting assignments/cancellations and shortlist edits save immediately. - IndexedDB upgrades in place, retaining the existing current-save string and preserving it as a legacy checkpoint on the first new write. Each save atomically writes the snapshot, increments the revision and prunes excess checkpoints. The checkpoint journal is independent of the portable season version and validates snapshots before writing.
- Each tab writes against its observed revision. Another tab's newer save causes a conflict instead of an overwrite; browsing history does not silently accept the change. Explicitly loading a checkpoint permits subsequent saves from that point. This is conflict protection rather than an exclusive writer lease.
- Damaged current payloads open recovery history, and invalid checkpoint rows cannot be restored. Failed writes keep prior saves intact and show a persistent message while the live game remains available for retry/export. The five checkpoints are shared across the single local season slot; named career slots and automatic saving after every match action remain future work.

## Implemented: persistent health foundation

Injury incidence remains uncalibrated. Severely depleted squads retain the development engine's existing two-player threshold: 2-4 eligible players may start; fewer than two produces a default at zero exposure. ## Explicit limits of this milestone

This is a FIBA 2024 exhibition subset, not certification for all planned competitions. EuroLeague has verified differences in jump balls, period possession and technical-foul totals. The [rules evidence matrix](design/rules-coverage.md) separates tested scenarios from partial or missing coverage.

Technical/unsportsmanlike/disqualifying fouls, combined penalties, reviews, lane violations, goaltending, separately timed release/flight adjudication, three-/five-/eight-second violations, all throw-in locations and the late-game frontcourt advance choice remain unimplemented. Possession creation and shot resolution remain discrete actions. Authoritative spacing and assigned defenders now affect outcomes, but continuous movement, collisions, screens, help rotations, switches and zone defense remain unimplemented. The current three-point classification uses basket distance and authored perimeter positions that avoid the corner-line exception; full arbitrary-location court adjudication remains open. The deterministic injury and fatigue models, tactical coefficients and player strength require statistical calibration.

The fictional development league supports consecutive seasons, employment expiry, salary/term offers and offseason free-agent recruitment. There is no configured real domestic/European league, complete career model, game-ready historical database, full commercial/facilities system, complete training/development model, academy, full staff-role system, regional scouting networks, board/press or detailed rehabilitation yet. The sixty-player population has calendar-derived ages, but no age-dependent development/performance, retirement or replenishment. Training and coaching can improve skill ratings within their development cap. Training-coach hiring uses both a season allocation and available cash when finances are enabled. Ticketing, a cash ledger and annual salary allocations are implemented; midseason moves, negotiated release clauses, loans, agents, facilities and full board finances remain open. Unimplemented management systems are omitted from navigation. Acquired reference records have not been converted into career squads or skill ratings. The first-release requirement remains twelve domestic divisions and three European competitions.

Watched and skipped managed matches still run on the main thread; matchday completion, recruitment-calendar advancement and season rollover run in a cancellable worker. Local revision recovery, matchday/recruitment-calendar/contract/scouting-decision autosaves and stale-write rejection are implemented. Named career slots, saving after every match action and exclusive multi-tab writer leases remain future work. Season and exhibition saves are separate development formats, not stable long-term career formats. Format-1 prototype matches cannot be resumed under either supported engine.
